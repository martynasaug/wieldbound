3D assets used by the Three.js client (Phase 47 onward). All CC0 (public
domain equivalent); no attribution is legally required, but provenance is
recorded here so every asset stays traceable — same policy as the 2D art in
../assets/ASSET_CREDITS.txt.

CHARACTERS + WEAPONS

Warrior.fbx            Quaternius "RPG Characters" (Nov 2020)
Ranger.fbx             https://opengameart.org/content/lowpoly-rpg-characters
Wizard.fbx             https://quaternius.com/  (CC0 1.0 Universal)
Monk.fbx
Rogue.fbx              6 rigged, animated, textured fantasy characters, all on
                       ONE shared skeleton — every bone within about a unit in
Warrior_Sword.fbx      three hundred of its counterpart on the other bodies.
Ranger_Bow.fbx         That is what lets one set of armour fit all of them, and
Ranger_Arrow.fbx       one weapon socket serve every class.
Rogue_Dagger.fbx
Wizard_Staff.fbx       Clip lists differ per body and the differences matter:
Cleric_Staff.fbx       the Warrior has Sword_Attack, the Wizard Staff_Attack,
                       the Ranger Bow_Attack_Draw/Shoot, the Rogue
                       Dagger_Attack, the Monk plain Attack. All five share
                       Idle, Walk, Run, RecieveHit, Roll, PickUp and Death, and
                       all but the Monk add Idle_Weapon.

                       The rig ships a dedicated `WeaponR` socket bone with
                       the weapon already parented to it. That is what makes
                       "your class is whatever you are holding" a mesh swap
                       on one bone, with every animation carrying it for free.

WHICH BODY IS WHICH CLASS  (see client/src/three/gear.ts)
  adventurer = Monk      warrior = Warrior
  ranger     = Ranger    mage    = Wizard

  Rogue.fbx is not used as a body — it is loaded only to harvest its dagger,
  which is the one weapon no other rig in the pack carries. Cleric.fbx is not
  shipped at all: the wand is the Wizard's staff at half length, so nothing
  needed it.

  Demon                demon          Goleling_Evolved golem
  Dragon_Evolved       dragon

Clips: Idle, Walk, Run, Punch, HitReact, Death, Jump, Duck, Wave, No.
Mapped to the engine's idle/run/attack/hit/die states in three/Actor.ts.

WHY glTF RATHER THAN THE FBX IN THE SAME PACK
  ~2.7x smaller for the identical model (Orc: 1250 KB vs 3395 KB), the texture
  atlas is embedded in a bufferView so each file is standalone, and it needs
  none of the material-rebuilding and UV-flip correction the FBX path below
  requires. The client loads both formats; `name` carries the extension.

  This pack is distributed from a Google Drive folder, which cannot be fetched
  programmatically — it was downloaded manually. Worth knowing if these ever
  need re-acquiring.

NATURE  (nature/*.gltf)

Quaternius "Stylized Nature MegaKit", CC0 1.0 Universal.
https://opengameart.org/content/stylized-nature-megakit

  41 models: 16 trees (CommonTree, Pine, TwistedTree, DeadTree), 3 boulders,
  5 pebbles, 2 bushes, 4 grasses, clover, fern, plants, flowers and mushrooms.

  WHAT USES WHAT
    Resource nodes  tree -> CommonTree/Pine, rock -> Rock_Medium,
                    bush -> Bush_Common_Flowers      (three/Game.ts NODE_MODELS)
    Treeline        every tree, outside the play bounds  (three/World.ts)
    Ground cover    everything ankle-height, instanced   (three/scatter.ts)

  Bush_Common is textured with the kit's TWISTED tree leaves, which are a deep
  autumnal red. It is treeline decor rather than the herb bush for that reason —
  as a harvestable it reads as dead scrub.

  Unlike the monsters pack, this one IS fetchable programmatically: it is a
  single zip served directly from opengameart.org.

  The textures were downscaled from 2048 to 512 by
  tools/art/shrink_nature_textures.ps1 — they are stylised palette atlases on
  low-poly meshes, and at this camera 2048 is about sixteen times more texel
  than any of it can resolve. 42 MB of PNG became 4.4 MB with no visible
  difference at any zoom the camera allows.

PROPS  (props/*.gltf)

Quaternius "Fantasy Props MegaKit", CC0 1.0 Universal.
https://opengameart.org/content/fantasy-props-megakit

  6 models forming the crafting station's smithy: Anvil_Log, Workbench,
  WeaponStand, Barrel, Crate_Wooden, Whetstone.  (three/Game.ts buildStation)

  This kit shares three "trim" atlases across every prop — furniture, metal and
  props — each with a BaseColor, Normal and ORM (occlusion/roughness/metalness)
  map. So six models cost nine textures rather than eighteen, and adding more
  props from the same kit costs no new textures at all.

  Also downscaled 2048 -> 512 by tools/art/shrink_kit_textures.ps1.

  Fetchable programmatically, like the nature kit: one zip from opengameart.org.

GROUND  (../textures/terrain/*.jpg)

Poly Haven, CC0. Fetched by tools/art/terrain.mjs.
  grass_*  <- leafy_grass      dirt_*  <- dirt

  Three maps each at 1k: diffuse, OpenGL-convention normal, and "arm" — which
  packs ambient occlusion, roughness and metalness into R, G and B. three.js
  reads exactly those channels, so one image serves as both roughnessMap and
  metalnessMap. The nor_gl variant matters: nor_dx has its green channel
  inverted and would light every bump from the wrong side.

  1k is deliberate. The ground tiles every 6 world units, so resolution buys
  nothing past one tile — what stops it looking repetitive is the macro
  variation three/terrain.ts mixes in, not a larger source image.

TEXTURES

../textures/*.png      Shipped with the packs above. Material names mostly
                       match file names; the loader resolves exact, then
                       `<name>_Texture`, then a prefix match, and falls back
                       to a flat colour (see assets.ts).

NOTE ON flipY

Textures keep three.js's default flipY (true). The usual advice for FBX is to
force it false — doing that here put every UV island on the wrong part of the
atlas and rendered the warrior's steel plate as the brown leather sitting
directly above it in the sheet.

WEAPONS  (weapons/*.fbx)

Quaternius "LowPoly Medieval Weapons" (Sept 2018), CC0 1.0 Universal.
https://opengameart.org/content/lowpoly-medieval-weapons

  23 models: 5 swords, 3 axes, 2 hammers, 2 daggers, 4 bows, a spear, a scythe,
  5 shields and an arrow. Fetched by tools/art/weapons.mjs — one zip served
  directly by OpenGameArt, so unlike the Ultimate Monsters pack this one is
  reproducible from a script.

  NO TEXTURES, and that is why this pack rather than a better-looking one. Every
  mesh is flat material colour under a small shared vocabulary — Steel,
  DarkSteel, LightSteel, Wood, DarkWood, Gold, Black and a few accents — which
  maps onto the material ROLES in three/gear.ts. So an item's palette can repaint
  a mesh and its quality can tint it, both without touching a UV. That is what
  makes 23 models into a 78-item catalogue (see shared/items.ts).

  The FBX are copied through unconverted: 835 KB for all 23, and the client
  already loads FBX for the character rigs.
